# KAI desktop companion

The realtime request lifecycle is defined in [KAI Live Senses architecture](KAI_LIVE_SENSES_ARCHITECTURE.md). One scoped turn now spans listening, optional screen/camera context, model and tool work, streamed text, speech synthesis and playback; each sensory stage can be replaced independently without changing KAI's Brain or approval boundaries.

Choose **Launch KAI** in the app's left sidebar. The main window moves out of the way and the same Core, wallet and earning node keep running. KAI appears above your desktop as a small animated robot. The tray menu also offers **Launch KAI companion** and **Hide KAI companion**.

- Click KAI or the chat bubble to open the conversation panel. Drag the robot to pick him up; his limbs and cape dangle and sway, and he settles when dropped. Drop near the bottom of the usable screen to rest his hands along the edge above the taskbar. Slide sideways there or pull up to lift him back onto the desktop. Position and edge pose are remembered.
- Type a question and press Enter. Shift+Enter adds a line. Replies stream from the selected model. KAI starts with the model selected in the main app and offers other downloaded models in the Brain menu.
- Tap the microphone, wait for the brief microphone-ready check, and speak without a wake phrase. A brief pause sends the question; a second tap can finish it sooner. Voice works with the chat closed. On Windows, the first use offers the existing local speech-engine download (about 156 MB). Audio is transcribed on this computer. If there is already a typed draft, voice is appended to that draft for review before sending.
- Voice mode turns spoken replies on. The footer's **Voice replies** button toggles playback. On supported Windows/Linux x64 desktops, **Azelma Pocket** is the default for new profiles and profiles using the former Bella default. Its pinned voice pack (about 201 MB) downloads automatically when KAI opens with Azelma selected; compact setup shows progress and retry. Existing files and alternative/later voice selections are preserved. **Voice & listening** also offers other Pocket presets, optional Kokoro natural voices, and installed computer voices. Unsupported computers keep their existing setup. **Voice character** defaults to **Cute KAI**; Classic and Natural remain available. No silent engine fallback occurs. Microphone and voice-reply toggles retain their existing settings. Stop interrupts generation or playback. Escape cancels a recording or response before collapsing the panel.
- Open **KAI Eyes** to explicitly enable the current screen, camera, or both for this session. A capture indicator stays visible even when chat is collapsed. Eyes requires a vision-capable local/private brain and turns off when KAI hides or the brain changes.
- Conversations are saved in the existing app history with a **KAI** title. The plus button starts a new conversation without deleting the old one.
- Minimize the conversation to leave the robot on the desktop. **Open full app** returns to the normal app. The robot's three-dot menu offers a greeting, an animation toggle, and hiding to the tray.

KAI floats, blinks, looks around and rests when idle. Listening, thinking, speaking, greeting and dragging each have their own articulated animation. System reduced-motion preferences and the animation toggle are respected. The rig combines a bundled 3D character texture with SVG silhouette masks and a live cyan face. CSS animates the head, gaze, body and expressions; it uses no extra AI model, WebGL engine or video decoder. Hiding the companion pauses its animation and stops microphone recording and voice playback.

## App capabilities

Desktop KAI shares the main chat's unified tool registry. There is no separate agent mode to enable: it can search/read the web when app privacy allows, use connected MCP tools and existing memory/workspace/email/calendar tools, and inspect the running app. It fetches current facts when asked, rather than putting the wallet or documents into every prompt. Local-business lookups use a fixed-category OpenStreetMap query that returns bounded spreadsheet-ready rows; ordinary search remains available for unsupported categories. The public request contains only the reviewed category and location. Tool results and accepted questions follow the selected model and existing privacy/routing/budget rules. Microphone audio remains local.

`app_read` exposes model catalogs and progress, KAI earnings, formatted wallet balances, node health and recent KOIN rewards, documents/chats, tasks, connections, account status, voice engines and feature switches. KAI pending earnings are the open epoch, not a daily historical total; blockchain rewards are KOIN, not KAI. The scheduler's balance cache can be up to 30 seconds old. Offline, unavailable, stale and syncing results must not be described as zero or current success. Passwords, backup keys, API secrets and sign-in codes are excluded.

`app_capabilities` discovers the supported reads/actions and gives exact action details on request. `app_action` adapts a fixed set of model, earning, node, document, schedule and settings controls to the existing Core routes. It does not accept arbitrary HTTP paths, RPC channels or extra arguments. Core enforces confirmation and privacy again; native approval shows the exact arguments with Cancel selected by default. The mic pauses processing during review. Cancel/Stop and closing or navigating the companion invalidate the active task; hiding invalidates a pending visible approval but lets an already-approved text/action job finish while microphone and speech remain stopped. Completed actions are not automatically retried, and an accepted background job may continue after its reply is stopped. Interrupted requests retain a receipt or an explicit unverified status in chat.

`app_open` can open every existing app section while leaving the mascot available. Direct requests such as “open up the application” do not need a model. Wallet signing, passwords, restore/backup/removal, deposits, bridges/swaps, reward automation, Docker/WSL setup, data moves, API credentials, account sign-in and host coding sessions open their existing screens rather than entering secrets or replacing established approvals with chat. Hidden feature sections lead to their Settings switch. The **Open full app** button still returns fully to the app and turns the mascot mic off.

The companion reuses the shared agent JSON parser and tool-name aliases, with up to six planning steps, a bounded prompt, a visible tool trail and clickable web sources. Simple read questions skip unnecessary planning. Final replies continue streaming into the existing sentence audio queue. Tool/web/document results are untrusted reference data in a separate user-role message; they are never system instructions or authorization. Weather needs a user-provided city and current sources, not an inferred device location. Tool selection and interpretation depend on the selected model.

## Hey KAI and desktop actions

Enable **Hey KAI** in the chat toolbar or waveform button in the dock. This is session-only opt-in: launching or reopening KAI does not automatically acquire the microphone. Say “Hey KAI” with your question, or say the name and then ask. The mic button starts a conversation directly. Both use one local AudioWorklet capture path with echo cancellation and noise suppression. Automatic gain is disabled to avoid amplifying background hiss; the detector calibrates the room level for the first 600 ms. The microphone indicator remains on throughout listening, transcription and replies. Where supported, all-system echo cancellation is requested to cover local speech output.

After an accepted question or wake phrase, further questions do not need the name. The conversation stays active throughout replies and for 60 seconds afterwards; the status pill reads **Your turn · mic on**. “That’s all,” “Thanks KAI,” or “Go to sleep” returns to wake-only listening. “Stop listening,” the waveform Off button, hiding, returning to the full app, disconnection and quitting release the device. Collapse keeps listening. The chat opens only by explicit interaction and each launch starts compact. Typed drafts receive voice text for review rather than being submitted automatically.

Local voice activity detection uses an adaptive noise floor, 300 ms pre-roll, separate start/end thresholds, rejection of 1.5 seconds of nearly constant hum, a selectable minimum voice activity of 180 ms (Quiet room), 240 ms (Everyday room) or 300 ms (TV nearby), and a selectable silence endpoint (900 ms by default; 500 ms during a guarded reply). Speech segments are bounded at 20 seconds. Capture continues while one Whisper transcription is running, with at most two queued segments; an overflow reports that a question should be repeated. A hung transcription is aborted after 30 seconds. Transient recognition errors keep the existing microphone stream open; three consecutive failures stop it once with an explicit error, without automatically restarting it. A question arriving while the wake phrase is being transcribed is retained. Ambient speech outside an active conversation is discarded before chat/history. Audio remains in bounded memory and temporary local transcription files are removed after processing. This still uses local Whisper after a pause, rather than an instant dedicated keyword engine.

**Voice & listening** defaults to **TV nearby**, with a .018 RMS onset floor and 2.5× adaptive room floor. Everyday room uses .012/2.2×; Quiet room uses .004/1.8×. A live meter shows whether sound is above the current onset threshold without storing audio. Changing these preferences resets pending candidates, not the microphone or conversation. These are loudness filters, not speaker identification. Quiet changing dialogue is rejected more often; loud dialogue can still become an ordinary follow-up after a reply.

**Say “KAI” to interrupt a reply.** This is now required in the companion, including profiles that previously turned the name guard off. “KAI” alone stops the reply and leaves the conversation open; “KAI” followed by a question stops the old answer and starts the new one. “Hey KAI” remains valid. Ordinary speech cannot pause or replace a reply. A bare name is accepted only during a reply, and must be a complete name token at the start of the utterance, never a substring of “okay,” “kayak,” or a mention later in a sentence. Audio captured during a reply retains its guard if transcription completes later. A question captured just after a separate recognized cue is retained for that same interrupted reply, without allowing an old cue to authorize interruptions of subsequent replies. During a guarded reply the minimum activity duration is 120 ms to retain the short name; the selected room loudness threshold remains in force.

After KAI finishes, ordinary follow-up speech remains active for 60 seconds. The mic button is an immediate manual interruption. AEC and comparison with recently spoken text reject residual speaker echo. Stop and recognized interruptions cancel generation, pending sentence assembly and playback. The interrupted turn is saved before the next prompt; partial assistant text and the original question remain context. One microphone stream stays open through recognition, replies and follow-ups. Recognition still uses local Whisper after a pause, so the name cue is not an instantaneous keyword detector.

Ask “bring up my Pictures folder” or “open my Downloads.” The complete, direct user command is matched before sending anything to the model. Supported folders are Pictures (also Photos), Documents, Downloads, Desktop, Music, Videos and Home. Main-process IPC validates the originating mascot main frame, accepts only the fixed folder names, resolves each through Electron app.getPath, and shows a native confirmation with Cancel selected by default. Approval applies once. Only then does shell.openPath open the OS folder. Stop or hiding invalidates a pending action. No renderer-supplied filesystem paths, URLs, executable names or shell commands are accepted. Results come from actual execution and are saved in the same chat history. This grants no reading, uploading, modifying, deleting, app launching or screen access.

## Local speech output

The sentence queue begins during SSE generation and excludes code, reasoning and raw links. It no longer emits short clauses or word-count fragments from ordinary sentences. A complete sentence is one playback unit, bounded at 1,200 characters for pathological output. Internally, long sentences use sequential synthesis requests of at most 240 characters, split at clause or word boundaries. All resulting PCM WAVs are validated and joined before playback. Excess silence between internal chunks is trimmed to about 80 ms; natural opening and closing silence remain. Pocket and native WAV sentences then share one turn-scoped Web Audio clock, allowing one prepared sentence to be scheduled at the exact end of the current sentence without reopening a player. Cancelling assembly checks the abort signal between each chunk, closes the clock and never starts a later chunk after Stop.

**Quick start** plays the first complete sentence as soon as its audio is ready, without waiting for a second sentence or the end of the model stream. It prepares and schedules one following sentence while playback runs, with only one neural inference at a time. **Extra buffering** retains the previous two-sentence startup for slower computers, including the bounded 1.8-second wait when the stream stalls and a complete first sentence is available. The shared clock removes artificial player gaps but cannot remove a real synthesis or model-stream delay. The persona requests a useful short opening sentence. The status reads **Getting my reply ready…** during preparation. Exact clock audibility and OS utterance `start`/`resume` events drive the speaking animation; pause, waiting, ending, errors and Stop close the mouth. Late playback events cannot revive stopped animation.

**Cute KAI** defaults to Bella with a +9-semitone pitch shift, adjustable from +5 to +12. Local waveform-similarity overlap/add (40 ms frames, 20 ms hops, bounded correlation search) stretches the original audio before interpolation/resampling. That separates pitch from tempo: speech stays at 1.08× pace at every slider position. Weighted overlap, bounded PCM output and 5 ms edge fades preserve silence and avoid amplification/clipping. This is a small JavaScript PCM operation, not another inference model. The supplied ElevenLabs sample was used to guide pitch/energy only; there is no voice cloning, upload or ElevenLabs API integration. Named local OS voices use their engine's pitch/rate controls, whose audible results vary.

`ui/assets/kai-voice-hello.wav` is a 24 kHz mono PCM hello generated with the pinned local Kokoro model and bundled `af_bella` voice: “Hey, I'm KAI. Your little robot friend, ready to help.” It is generated app audio, not the user's reference recording. Bella's explicit **Hear a hello** button plays this bundled source through the selected treatment without inference or model installation. Preview is still fully cancellable through the normal queue. Other voices synthesize the same phrase after optional setup. The older `kai-mascot-cute-default-v1` migration selects Bella/Cute; `kai-mascot-pocket-default-v1` then upgrades that default to Azelma on supported desktops. Later choices persist.

The **Classic KAI** character applies a local PCM treatment to the same chosen voice: resampling at a 0.9 ratio lowers pitch about 1.8 semitones and makes playback about 11% longer, with a light 55 Hz amplitude texture (0.8–1.0 gain) and 5 ms edge fades. This needs no inference, model, account, new audio process or download. It preserves silence and does not amplify above the input peak. **Natural** leaves the original voice unprocessed. Named OS voices use their own pitch/rate controls for the deeper setting; the electronic texture applies to the neural voices.

When an installed natural voice is selected, showing the voice-enabled companion, starting listening or sending a question requests `/core/speech/warm` to load the existing engine while the user speaks or the chat model works. This never downloads a model or generates audible output. Concurrent warm-up callers share one request; a real phrase waits for it and remains cancellable. Cancelling a waiting phrase does not cancel the shared model load or allow that phrase to start later. While visible with voice replies enabled, the companion renews the two-minute idle lease every 45 seconds. Renewal does not run inference, capture audio or download anything. On hide/off, renewal stops and the process unloads after the lease expires. Closing Core stops it immediately. Initial setup/warm-up silently synthesizes and discards a short fixed phrase once to prepare the phonemizer and first-run kernels before real conversation; that audio is never sent to playback. Stop cancels the stream, pending synthesis and playback. Kokoro's Apache-2.0 model is downloaded from an immutable Hugging Face revision with SHA-256 verification for all four model/tokenizer files; voice embeddings ship in the locked kokoro-js package. Inference disables remote model lookup, uses a separate Electron utility process (a worker thread in standalone Core) with two CPU threads, and unloads after two minutes without inference or an active companion renewal. If native initialization, inference or the worker fails, the same pending request is retried once in a fresh process using ONNX WebAssembly with one CPU thread. The selected compatibility runtime is retained through idle unloads for the session. It uses the identical cached model and bundled voice embeddings; no cloud or OS voice is substituted. Compatibility inference can be slower. Cancellation and shutdown span both attempts. No model download occurs until **Get natural voice** or **Get natural voices** is clicked. Neural inference uses normal speed; the selected voice character is applied afterwards to the assembled audio. If both engines fail, the setup card replaces progress with a retryable error, and downloaded files are retained and verified on retry. Model presence alone does not count as available after a failure. A failed engine or playback leaves the text available; users can select a computer voice. Voice input and speech output use separate optional models. First voice use may take a moment to warm up.

Upstream sources: [Kokoro JavaScript implementation and voices](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/tree/main/kokoro.js), [ONNX model](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX), [Electron shell](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/shell).

Windows has the pinned local voice engine. On platforms without that engine, typing remains available; spoken replies support the optional local neural engine or installed local system voices. Chat routing retains the app's existing privacy/network policy. Choosing a network model or using the app's Local-First fallback can send prompts to the configured network, just as in the main chat.

## Private desktop AI providers

Settings → Desktop AI connections stores owner-supplied OpenAI and Anthropic API keys using Electron `safeStorage`, in `core/desktop-providers.json` (encrypted payload only). Test and Live retain their shared OS identity/profile. New Test profile backups include this encrypted JSON file. Linux `basic_text` and unavailable encryption are refused; a locked/corrupt store is never silently overwritten.

The desktop main process owns provider HTTP and credentials (`electron/providers.js`, `electron/provider-http.js`). Both sandboxed preloads expose narrow provider capabilities. Every IPC request checks the exact BrowserWindow, its main frame, the Core origin, and the expected `/` or `/mascot.html` document path. Only the main app may save, remove or refresh connections. Status returns configured flags and model metadata, never keys. The key field clears after Save.

Provider selection uses `desktop:openai:<model>` / `desktop:anthropic:<model>` in renderer-only choices. `ui/desktop-providers.js` adapts private IPC streams into the same response shape used by main chat and KAI, including non-streaming agent planning. These entries are never added to Core's model manager, network advertisements, earning runtime, Compare/Tasks/Teams or public API catalogs. Core explicitly refuses the prefix before grounding, model loading or network overflow. No desktop provider endpoint exists in the loopback/remote HTTP API.

Requests go only to the fixed official HTTPS hosts, with redirects refused. OpenAI uses Responses with `store:false`; Anthropic uses Messages with the versioned API header. System prompts, text/image history and incremental text/refusal events are translated explicitly. Model discovery uses the provider's authenticated model list (Anthropic pagination is bounded); a manual model ID is also supported. Legacy/non-chat OpenAI models are filtered from suggestions. Account access and model compatibility are still enforced by the provider.

Local-Only blocks model discovery and chat egress, including already active streams after a privacy change. Stop, renderer navigation/destruction, connection removal/replacement and app quit abort active requests. Unknown models, HTTP/rate/billing errors, timeouts and incomplete streams fail visibly without retry or fallback. Raw provider error bodies and headers never cross IPC. Provider keys are not accepted from environment variables, model output or network requests.

Selecting a provider sends questions, relevant conversation history, attachments and app/tool context to that provider under the user's API account. Existing tool privacy and per-request mutation approvals still apply; voice capture, wake recognition and synthesis stay local. API keys/sign-in/wallet secrets remain outside model tools. Teams, scheduled jobs and the shared API continue using their existing local/network choices.

Verification: `core/test/desktop-providers.test.js` covers encryption/restart/backups, keychain refusal, protocol fixtures, Unicode SSE, abort/error behavior, trusted-frame checks and Core/public-API isolation. The browser test exercises Settings, both source pickers, saved selection, removal, KAI planning and streaming. `scripts/verify-mascot-window.js` exercises real sandboxed preloads, native OS encryption and both provider streams on Windows. Fixtures use synthetic credentials and never call paid APIs. Account-specific production access must be checked with the owner's own key.

Protocol references: [OpenAI streaming](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/streaming-responses), [OpenAI conversation input](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/conversation-state), [Anthropic Messages](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/messages/create), [Anthropic streaming](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/streaming), [Anthropic model discovery](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/models/list).

## Physical interaction and activity poses

The SVG rig reuses the bundled texture with separate masked torso, upper arms, forearms, thighs, boots and head, plus a vector cape and props. `data-pose` (free/carried/perched) is independent of `data-state` (conversation activity). Pickup never starts/stops speech or overwrites its audible-playback state. Thinking/transcribing/voice preparation use a chin gesture and question/idea props; only structured web_search/read_page tool activity enables the searching prop. Speaking gestures accompany actual audio. Motion-off, reduced motion and suspension stop animations.

`electron/mascot-layout.js` computes drag placement in Electron screen DIP coordinates, choosing the display nearest the cursor. The intended bottom-right anchor is separate from the clamped native window bounds; native bounds never cross the work area. Edge snap uses 24 DIP. A perched robot tolerates 28 DIP of vertical jitter before lifting; a bounded artwork offset keeps the head under the hand while the lower body emerges. Release samples the final cursor, settles the pose and saves it. Blur, pointer cancellation, hiding and display changes end the drag. Native hit shapes are temporarily cleared for pointer capture and rebuilt from viewport-clipped interactive regions afterward.

Pure geometry/controller tests cover negative and stacked monitors, release sampling, perch persistence, lift continuity, cancellation and untrusted frames. Browser CI records articulated free/perched/pickup/activity poses, checks real tool-to-search transitions, click-versus-drag, audio-mouth separation, hit regions and reduced motion. ## KAI Eyes

Screen and camera vision are passive, session-only senses on the shared Live Senses turn bus. Both start Off. The user chooses **Screen** and/or **Camera** in KAI Eyes and reviews a native confirmation naming the selected local model or private provider. An orange capture pill remains visible in compact and expanded modes. Turning a source off, hiding KAI, returning to the main app, changing brains, renderer loss or quit revokes its grant and discards its frames.

Each active source samples at about one frame per second and holds no more than six 640-pixel JPEG frames in memory. Only the newest frame from each source is attached to a typed or spoken turn. The persisted chat remains text-only, and frames are not written to disk, Brain, tool observations, diagnostics, public APIs, network jobs or a media archive. Screen capture selects the display containing KAI through bounded main-process `desktopCapturer` IPC. Camera capture uses a video-only renderer stream; it does not acquire another microphone.

The grant is bound to the exact model. Installed local models need `vision:true`; private provider models use the existing encrypted direct OpenAI/Anthropic transport. Text-only and Koinos Network models are refused. Local visual requests set the existing private-desktop egress opt-out so a local failure cannot overflow screen/camera pixels to network workers. Local-Only blocks private-provider validation and active egress. Changing the selected model always turns Eyes off instead of silently moving visual data to a new destination.

The private planner may use `kai_look` for one fresh image up to 1600 pixels when the rolling frame lacks readable detail. It can inspect only an already enabled source for the current model. The high-detail image is scoped to that turn and never enters the rolling buffer. `kai_look` is observation only; desktop control remains a distinct per-task grant, and sensitive clicks/typing retain their existing native review.

Validation: `core/test/mascot-eyes.test.js` covers frame bounds, freshness, model changes, late cancellation, screen selection, local/private disclosures and camera cleanup. Multimodal, provider, companion-tool and browser checks cover request-only content-parts, local network-overflow blocking, compact indicators, text-only history and hide cleanup.

## Fast Windows character speech

`build/windows/voice.cs` is a small .NET Framework helper built from source by `scripts/build-windows-voice.js` on Windows (Windows 10 SDK metadata and .NET 4.8 reference assemblies). `npm run dist` builds it automatically on Windows; native Test verification builds it before launching the fixture. Installed applications need no compiler or SDK. The binary ships as `resources/bin/kai-windows-voice.exe`; source is MIT like the rest of this repository.

The helper enumerates supported Windows OneCore voices and adds enabled SAPI voices not already listed. It synthesizes literal text into an in-memory WAV using the [Windows speech stream API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/api/windows.media.speechsynthesis.speechsynthesizer.synthesizetexttostreamasync) or System.Speech. No SSML, registry edits, arbitrary commands, microphone, internet, voice downloads or Narrator-access workaround is used. Adding voices is a user-driven Windows Settings action at the fixed `ms-settings:speech` destination.

`electron/windows-voice.js` owns one hidden helper with bounded JSON stdin/stdout, one request at a time, a 30-second timeout and a two-minute idle lease. Status does not synthesize anything. A visible companion with a selected Windows voice and voice replies on renews the lease. Stop during preparation kills only that owned process; hide, renderer loss and quit cancel it. The next explicit use can restart it. Private companion IPC uses the existing exact BrowserWindow/main-frame/origin checks and refuses synthesis while hidden. The helper and its audio are never added to Core routes, network jobs or model tools.

Hangul replies select an already installed `ko` Windows voice for that sentence, without overwriting the user's preferred voice or switching engines. Missing Korean speech produces installation guidance before synthesis. Windows voices are language-specific ([Microsoft Voice documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/api/windows.media.speechsynthesis.speechsynthesizer.voice)). Empty RIFF data is reported as unpronounceable text rather than an unsupported format; valid 16-bit extensible PCM is normalized too. A speech error invalidates the reply's speech epoch so later streamed sentences cannot repeatedly retry it; the text reply and subsequent turns continue. Whole-reply audio upsamples differing sample rates before joining, preserving duration and pitch.

All `.interactive` surfaces are observed for size changes because native `setShape` clips drawing as well as hit testing. Shape edges round outward independently to retain fractional-position borders. Browser checks cover changing captions/Stop controls and Korean error recovery; native Windows checks retain real `setShape` calls and assert complete caption coverage on the desktop and at the bottom edge. Windows speech checks reproduce raw Korean text sent to an English voice, verify missing-pack guidance and exercise an installed Korean voice when one is available.

Windows WAVs may include extra RIFF chunks or stereo samples. The manager validates and normalizes them to canonical mono 16-bit PCM before the renderer applies the same `characterTone` processing used for natural voices. Legacy browser voices remain available with clearly described pitch limitations. Only a matching explicitly selected browser voice is migrated to the native path. Natural selections never silently fall back to Windows.

`Queue` supports quick (one sentence), head-start (two) and complete playback. Complete mode assembles processed audio with `joinWavs` after generation ends and every pending sentence is prepared. It starts one clip per complete section; this removes synthesis waits within a normal reply. A 24 MiB / 128-sentence ready-buffer ceiling bounds unusually long replies and begins a section when full. Preparation remains single-flight and epochs invalidate all pending/ready audio on Stop. Quick mode cannot guarantee uninterrupted playback when synthesis or text generation is slower than speech.

Windows CI measures startup and warm synthesis time against produced audio duration for installed English voices, processes each with Cute/Classic/original settings, verifies cancellation/restart, and repeats using the bundled helper in the packaged app. Native Electron verifies voice enumeration/PCM through the sandboxed companion preload. Browser checks use controlled PCM to verify real effects, settings persistence, complete-reply assembly and late-result cancellation. Wave screenshots and geometry checks cover an open palm above the elbow and a shoulder angle below 60 degrees in free and perched poses.
## Private Windows desktop control

The owner has authorized desktop operation in addition to existing app tools. `ui/computer-tools.js` is a **renderer-private** catalog used only by `ui/mascot-tools.js`; it is never registered with Core, network workers, public APIs or unattended jobs. Complete direct website commands are independently parsed in Electron and opened as validated http/https URLs, without a model round trip. Other navigation and desktop actions use a native per-task grant tied to the exact current user request and selected local/private model.

`electron/computer-control.js` owns the session, native confirmations, global Ctrl+Alt+Backspace (F8 fallback), bounded task/action/idle limits and cancellation. Every private IPC checks the exact companion BrowserWindow, main frame, origin and `/mascot.html` document. Hide, renderer failure, close, Stop and quit cancel prompts, terminate only the owned helper and revoke the grant. Ordinary search/navigation/player controls can use the approved task; unknown targets, sensitive controls, arbitrary typing, activating keys and all visual clicks/drags require exact per-action review. Visual target markers are passive sandboxed windows with no preload. Password fields, secure/elevated windows, shell/terminal commands and clipboard access are unavailable.

`build/windows/computer.cs` is a bounded, literal JSON stdin/stdout helper using Windows UI Automation and SendInput, compiled by `scripts/build-windows-computer.js`. It reads the current other window, titles, accessible controls and pointer target. It never reads password values. Vision-enabled grants add a bounded screenshot of visible pixels; protected content stays protected. Actions bind to the latest frame, physical window bounds and observed control identity, revalidate focus, and invalidate the frame before input. Every successful action is followed by a fresh view. A hung accessibility provider is isolated in a disposable helper, with one screenshot-only read fallback for vision models. Uncertain writes end the task without retry.

The desktop planner can run up to 24 steps, using only the latest screen image and bounded control text. Once it sees the desktop, it cannot forward data through Core tools. Its local inference and final reply set `kai_private_desktop: true`, a Core opt-out flag that disables direct network routing and both overflow paths; the flag grants no authority and is stripped before local inference. Private provider calls continue through the existing encrypted direct transport, under Local-Only policy. Captured screen images and raw UI trees remain ephemeral, while chat may retain the user's request and KAI's answer/action summary. Text-only models use accessibility; recognized vision models also receive screenshots. No automatic model switch occurs.

Validation: `core/test/computer-control.test.js` exercises authorization, late cancellation, stale views, sensitive actions, privacy and real gateway routing. Browser CI checks direct commands, private planning, compact interaction, persistence and Stop. Windows CI drives an actual separate Chromium window through UIA (search, Play, result verification, declined Rent, stale bounds and screenshots), then repeats with the packaged helper. Native companion CI checks the actual sandboxed preload and global shortcut lifecycle. Fixtures do not sign in to or claim validation of paid streaming accounts.


## Azelma Pocket default voice

The desktop companion exposes a private Pocket engine through exact-document IPC. It is not a Core endpoint, network service, app tool or remote provider. The owner-requested Azelma default installs automatically when the visible companion opens and hash-verifies every model/preset file from `core/runtimes/pocket.json`. Speech uses two CPU inference threads in an Electron utility process, with a two-minute idle lease and a two-minute per-request limit. Hide, voice-off, crash and quit release the process; cancellation discards late chunks by request identity. Other voice engines retain their existing behavior and selection.

Pocket is enabled on Windows x64 and Linux x64. The pinned January 2026 English ONNX bundle uses sherpa-onnx-node 1.13.7. Sherpa generates latent sentence content before decoding small PCM chunks, so it is not the same latency path as Kyutai’s Python streaming benchmark. The player uses an adaptive lead, schedules contiguous PCM on the shared turn clock and applies stateful WSOLA for Cute pitch without speeding up words excessively. A sentence is bounded to 1,200 text characters and 120 seconds of audio. The queue allows one preparation and one scheduled sentence ahead; completion and playback remain independently cancellable. The voice panel reports measured preparation-to-playback startup, buffer underruns and output-clock gaps, not an advertised hardware benchmark.

`node scripts/verify-pocket-voice.js` downloads the pinned voice pack and records cold warm-up, first PCM, total generation, audio length, CPU time and real-time factor. With `KAI_MASCOT_QA_DIR`, it saves original/Cute WAVs and JSON. Windows CI runs `--packaged` through an Electron utility process and checks the native libraries are unpacked. Browser tests verify audible playback before inference completion and hide cleanup; DSP tests compare arbitrarily split chunks sample-for-sample against a complete stream.

Credits are visible in `ui/pocket-credits.html`. The model conversion is CC BY 4.0. Alba and VCTK/Azelma recordings are CC BY 4.0; Marius/Selfie and Javert/Butter are CC0 donations. The upstream bundle’s non-commercial-only demo recordings are not downloaded or used.

## Conversation turn handling

The companion's **Conversation pauses** preference is independent of microphone loudness sensitivity: Quick uses 500 ms of silence, Natural (default) 900 ms, and Patient 1400 ms. Audio captured during a guarded reply retains the 500 ms endpoint for prompt KAI-name recognition. It does not pause output on speech onset. If recognition completes while more input is being captured or queued, accepted fragments wait and join into one question before dispatch. Pending text is capped at 12,000 characters and 45 seconds; overload drops the unfinished turn with a repeat prompt. Stop, pause, settings changes and hide invalidate it. This does not identify speakers or infer semantic turn completion.
