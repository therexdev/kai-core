# "Run Koinos Node" in Koinos AI — design recommendation

> Produced 2026-08-18 by a design run over three codebases: two integration
> strategies (vendor-the-modules, shared-package) each attacked on maintenance
> burden and user reality. The companion-app strategy was ruled out beforehand by
> the owner's one-wallet decision. Claims marked verified below were re-checked by
> hand, not taken on trust.

Ranking: [{'name': 'Node Console (companion mode)', 'avg': 7.5, 'fatals': 6}, {'name': 'Koinos Mode — one wallet, one shared money library, two shells', 'avg': 7, 'fatals': 8}]

## In plain language

Here's what I'd build. A switch at the bottom of Earn called "Koinos node tools." Turn it on and a Koinos section appears in the sidebar with four things: your node's live numbers (paste any Koinos address — KOIN, VHP, mana); a box pointing at your node, which defaults to this machine, so if Koinos Node Desktop is running here it just connects and shows whether it's synced; an honest card about the node software — whether this computer can run one, what it really costs, and a button to get it; and your Koinos AI address, shown as a name, not a place to send money.

Turn it off and nothing happens but hiding. No timers, no downloads, no network calls, and it never starts or stops a real node.

What you get first is everything you can look at. What waits is everything that moves money. Burning KOIN to VHP and registering your producer key come in the next release, once I've added two safety pieces the app doesn't have yet. Sending comes after that. Card purchases stay in Koinos Node Desktop — putting them here means a big crypto library and copying your sponsor key into a far more widely downloaded app, for something that already works next door.

The node itself never runs inside Koinos AI. It can't on a Pi — that software isn't built for that chip at all — and on Windows it needs a reboot and a 600 MB install, which doesn't belong in a chat app.

Cost to you: under 100 KB on the installer, no new libraries, and one small file that mirrors the other app.

## Recommendation

NODE TOOLS — read-only first, connected second, never a container. Build from Node Console's structural refusals (nothing shared, nothing Docker, nothing ETH, capability gate before any button, watch-any-address), graft Koinos Mode's local-RPC connection card as the thing that makes the two apps complementary instead of redundant, and split the surface strictly by blast radius: stage 1 has zero write routes and asks for no password, so it cannot lose anyone a coin and needs none of the CSRF/auth/money-test machinery that would otherwise gate it. Writes (burn, producer registration) ship as a separate release whose entry price is three named prerequisites. Sending ships after that. The Docker tier, the ETH/onramp tier, a shared npm package, and auto-installing the node app are refused permanently, not deferred.

## Why this one

Node Console scored higher and deserved to: it refuses to duplicate the ~8,200 lines that actually contain bugs, adds zero npm dependencies, and its capability-gate-before-the-button pattern is the right shape for every hardware refusal in this app. But both judges landed the same three hits on it, and all three are real — I verified the worst one myself: core/lib/wallet.js exposes create/importWif/restore/unlock/lock/revealWif/signHash/remove and NO transfer, and gateway.js has no transfer route, so its "recommended" flow of buying in the node app and sending to the Koinos AI address instructs an irreversible one-way deposit into a wallet with no spend path. Koinos Mode scored lower but owns the single best idea in either design — a node RPC URL field defaulting to 127.0.0.1:8080, probed with chain_get_head_info, ~60 lines, which is the only thing in stage 1 that gives anything at all to the person already running a node (Node Console's emptiest screen, and that person flips the toggle first). It also owns the correct privacy chokepoint and the correct verdict that renderer.js must be rewritten, not pasted.

Where I diverge from both is the write surface. Koinos Mode puts send, burn and producer-registration in stage 1, into an app that shipped v0.3.7 to v0.27.5 in four days, on a control plane where _authed() is never called under /core/, grep for origin/cors/referer returns zero, and bodies are JSON-parsed regardless of content-type. That is the first money-moving, unauthenticated, CSRF-reachable endpoint in the app, and the only manual money test in the design is attached to a future PR in the other repo. Stage 1 with no writes and no password prompt needs no Origin check, no password-per-call, no harbinger CI, and no consent card — it ships in days, buys a full release of real exposure on the toggle/settings/privacy/poller plumbing, and fixes Koinos Mode's forgot-password dead end for free, because a user who can't produce their password loses nothing when nothing asks for it.

THE FLAW BOTH SHARE, PLAINLY: both treat the switch's name and the earn wallet's double life as copy problems solved by one card the user dismisses. They are structural. On most machines "Run Koinos Node" runs nothing — it reveals a wallet. And both drop live mainnet balance tiles into the same .api-card, under the same h1, roughly seventy lines below the sentence "Alpha runs on the Koinos testnet — rewards are test tokens" (ui/index.html:307), with nothing between them. A person who spent weeks learning "the numbers in this app are test tokens" carries that model straight into real money. Neither design ever shows a persistent answer to "is this number real?" — they show it once and move on. My answer: an honest label in stage 1 renamed when it becomes true, a separate view with its own nav item and a standing mainnet tag, and no receive framing anywhere.

## Stage 1 — implementable spec

STAGE 1 — "NODE TOOLS", READ-ONLY. No writes, no password prompt, no consent card, no irreversible anything. Roughly 2-3 focused days.

PREREQUISITES, in the same commit as the markup:
- ui/styles.css gains `#view-earn { overflow-y: auto }` (confirmed absent — grepping #view- returns only lines 85, 148, 600) and `#view-koinos { overflow-y: auto; padding: 34px 8% }`.
- No inline style attributes anywhere. kaiapp's CSP is style-src 'self' with no unsafe-inline, proven live by the already-dead style="color:#f0b35e" at ui/index.html:357. Classes, or el.style.foo from JS.

THE TOGGLE: inserted at ui/index.html line 376 — the blank line between `</div>` closing #earn-ready (375) and `<p class="error" id="earn-error">` (377, verified exactly). Outside #earn-ready, so it is visible at every wallet stage and so core/test/earn-ui.browser.test.js, which waits on #earn-setup / #earn-ready hidden-attribute transitions, is untouched. Not a checkbox — .check is scoped to .modal .check, so use the existing pattern: a button that flips `hidden`, exactly like #btn-earn-show-restore at app.js:1159. Static markup present at parse time, because every earn listener runs unconditionally at script load (app.js:1058 onward) and a null $() there aborts the rest of app.js.

STATE: settings.set("koinos.enabled", …) server-side via POST /core/koinos/config, default false. Off means INERT IN CORE, not merely hidden: GET /core/koinos returns {ok:true, enabled:false} and nothing else, no Provider is constructed, no timer is scheduled, no probe runs.

ON: a "Koinos" nav item appears and #view-koinos is revealed. Four cards (copy in uiPlan). The Earn tab keeps only the toggle and a one-line "Koinos tools are on — open them →". Deliberately not a wall of content inside Earn: #view-earn cannot scroll, and mainnet numbers must not sit inside the same .api-card as the "rewards are test tokens" lede at index.html:307.

CORE: a `koinos` service constructed in core/server.js and injected into Gateway alongside earn/network (gateway.js:69 destructures a fixed option list; add it there and at the call site ~server.js:385). Name it `koinos`, never `node` — `nodeRuntime` already exists in that list. It holds the vendored read-only chain client, which constructs ITS OWN koilib Provider and never touches core/lib/wallet.js's singleton `get signer()` (line 47), the object core/lib/worker.js:265 signs earn receipts with. Server-side cache 30s, same shape as the earnings cache at server.js:121-154.

VENDORED, ~100 KB total:
- core/lib/chain-read.js (~180 lines, adapted from koinos-node electron/lib/chain.js: resolveContracts via the get_contract_address system call, balances, headInfo, registeredPublicKey. No burn, no transfer, no signer parameter anywhere.)
- core/lib/chain-constants.js (~40 lines: rpcUrl, explorer URLs, fallback contract addresses, KOIN_DECIMALS. Nothing about image tags, ports, p2p seeds or the 60 GB backup.)
- core/lib/abi/pob-abi.json + token-abi.json (25 KB, verbatim)
Each carries a header comment naming its counterpart's absolute path in the other repo, in both directions, and stating that chain-read.js is an adapted fork (different koilib minor, no signer, half the methods) that no hash test can cover.

CAPABILITY GATE, as DATA not code: one node-requirements object {arch:["x64"], minRamGb:8, minFreeGbToRun:45, minFreeGbForQuickSync:100} with a header comment naming koinos-node's constants.js as its source and a "requirements last verified 2026-08-18" line rendered in the card. The day arm64 images ship it is a one-line edit. Disk is split, not flat: below 45 GB free = can't; 45-100 GB = can run one, but genesis sync only, expect days. Measure free space on the volume the node will actually use, name that disk in the copy, and do not silently use hardware.js's statfs(dataDir) — on Linux those are routinely different mounts.

COMPANION DETECTION, filesystem probe only, no execution: win32 %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\koinos-node-desktop\Koinos Node Desktop.exe then %PROGRAMFILES%\; darwin /Applications/Koinos Node Desktop.app then ~/Applications/; linux a Koinos-Node-Desktop-*.AppImage in ~/Applications, ~/.local/bin, ~/Downloads. Version from the packaged package.json or Info.plist. NO companionPath in any POST body — that is the Node Console gadget: /core/* has no _authed(), no Origin check and no content-type check, so any visited web page could retarget the Open button at an arbitrary local binary. Linux's unfound-AppImage case gets a main-process file picker instead (electron/preload.js already has the pickModelFile precedent), so the path never round-trips through an unauthenticated route.

OPEN AND GET: `openCompanion()` in electron/preload.js → a `companion:open` handler in electron/main.js that asks Core for the detected path itself (main.js:118 already fetches http://127.0.0.1:<port>/core/earn/nudge, so the pattern exists) and spawns it detached. "Get Koinos Node Desktop" is window.open to the GitHub releases page, which electron/main.js:105-106's setWindowOpenHandler already routes to shell.openExternal. Managed/catalog install is refused permanently, not deferred.

UI: ui/koinos-view.js, ~450 lines, written fresh in the ui/tools-view.js IIFE shape — local `$` and `esc`, an early `if (!$("view-koinos")) return;`, document.createElement rather than template literals. Self-rescheduling setTimeout (10s), gated on `!$("view-koinos").hidden`, plus the userIsBusy() guard from tools-view.js:32-36 that refuses to redraw while focus is inside an input in that view. Never setInterval(heartbeat, 5000).

TESTS: see testPlan. All of them run without Docker and without spending anything, because stage 1 spends nothing.

## What a Raspberry Pi user sees

Card C, state (i), shown on an arm64 machine, with no install button and no start button anywhere on the screen:

"This computer can't run a Koinos node. The node software is only published for Intel and AMD chips, and this is an ARM computer (Raspberry Pi) — it isn't that it would be slow, the software simply doesn't exist for this chip.

You can still watch a node running somewhere else. Paste its address in the box above, or point 'Node address' at it. A small cloud server that can run one costs about $6-15 a month, and there's a setup guide here →"

That is checkable, not a hedge: every one of the 11 image tags pinned in koinos-node's constants.js:48-60 publishes a single amd64/linux manifest with zero arm64 variants; the compose file carries no platform: key on any of its 12 services, so `docker compose up -d` fails at pull with "no matching manifest for linux/arm64/v8"; and the owner's own cloud/provision.sh:91-92 and cloud/provision-core.sh:81-82 already warn on non-x86_64. Forcing platform: linux/amd64 under qemu emulation would run a 3-second-block chain against a stall detector that declares a node dead after 8 flat minutes (node-manager.js:18) — a permanently stalling node, not a slow one. And even with images, a node wants a few GB of RAM and tens of GB of chain state 24/7 on a box already running llama.cpp inference (core/runtimes/catalog.json:2 — "Raspberry Pi / arm64 rides linux-arm64-cpu").

Everything else on the screen works normally on that Pi: cards A, B and D are identical to a desktop. The Pi is a first-class node dashboard; it is just not a node.

## The onramp: refused, and why

NO ONRAMP CODE IN KOINOS AI, and this is a rule rather than a stage number — the ETH tier is the change that actually doubles the maintenance burden, and giving it a stage number is a scheduling note, not protection.

Three reasons, in order of force:

1. A PARTIAL ONRAMP IS WORSE THAN NONE. fund:buyUrl asks the owner's serverless endpoint to mint a Coinbase session token for wallet.ethAddress, and the user buys real ETH on Ethereum mainnet into a Koinos-derived Ethereum address. That ETH is only useful afterwards through the bridge (Route B) or swap-then-bridge (Route C). "Just add a Buy button" therefore produces a user holding real ETH in an address Koinos AI has no code to spend from. It is all-or-nothing: ethers ^6.17.0 (absent from kaiapp entirely), ~10 modules, 27 of the 67 channels, 878 lines of renderer, and three concurrent job state machines with their own advancer timers (15s bridge, 8s Route C) running outside the request surface.

2. THE SPONSOR SECRET IS A CLIENT-SIDE CONSTANT AND SHIPPING IT WIDER BUYS NOTHING. ONRAMP_APP_KEY = "kkapp_…" is a plain string literal at koinos-node electron/main.js:35, sent as the x-koinoskit-app header and checked by onramp-endpoint/api/sponsor.js:65 against ONRAMP_SHARED_SECRET, with SPONSOR_RC_MAX defaulting to 5 KOIN per transaction. Putting that same literal in a second, far more widely downloaded binary multiplies the abuse surface against the owner's real sponsor wallet and gives him no way to tell the two apps apart or shut one off. The endpoint cannot distinguish them.

3. IT ALSO KEEPS A LOADED GUN UNLOADED. Because no eth.js ever enters kaiapp, the @noble/hashes trap never fires: koinos-node's eth.js:11 does require("@noble/hashes/sha256.js"), which resolves under its hoisted 1.8.0 but throws ERR_PACKAGE_PATH_NOT_EXPORTED under the 2.3.0 kaiapp hoists from koilib 9.3.0 (I confirmed the version on disk). Since wallet.js and the whole send tier require it transitively, an unpatched copy takes down the wallet at load, not at call.

WHAT THE USER GETS INSTEAD, in card D: their Koinos AI address shown as an identity with a copy button and the sentence "Koinos AI can't send from this address — don't send funds to it." Plus, in card C, a button that opens Koinos Node Desktop, where buying, bridging and burning already exist and already work. From stage 5 that button lands directly on its Fund tab.

I am explicitly deleting the cross-app "buy there, then send to your Koinos AI address" flow that one of the candidate designs recommended. There is no reason for KOIN to be in the Koinos AI wallet — core/server.js:222 confirms the scheduler operator pays mana, so the earn wallet needs neither KOIN nor mana to function — and I verified the wallet has no transfer method and the gateway has no transfer route. Following that advice is an irreversible one-way deposit for zero benefit.

IF IT EVER SHIPS, three preconditions: a DISTINCT per-app key (kkai_…) so one can be revoked without killing the other and so sponsor logs attribute spend correctly; per-address and per-day caps in sponsor.js, not just the per-transaction SPONSOR_RC_MAX (a per-tx cap does nothing against a loop); and sizing every cap as if the key were public, because it is extractable from any installer with a text editor. Also declare and PIN @noble/hashes and @noble/secp256k1 explicitly — koinos-node declares neither and relies on hoisting.

DEFERRED AND WORTH SAYING OUT LOUD: a ?to=<address> deep-link so the node app's onramp could deliver straight to a chosen address. That is a change inside koinos-node's fund flow, which this session cannot make.

## Residual risk — what this still gets wrong

WHAT THIS DESIGN STILL GETS WRONG, honestly.

1. THE OWNER MAY BE DISAPPOINTED ON DAY ONE AND NO COPY FIXES IT. He asked for the node menus inside Koinos AI. Stage 1 gives a console, not a node. If he flips the switch on his own x86_64 Windows desktop expecting the node functionality to appear, what he sees is four cards and a link to the other app. Only stage 5's interop file plus a Start button closes that gap, and stage 5 needs a repo this session cannot push to. Everything before it is a smaller promise, honestly labelled — but it is a smaller promise.

2. THE VENDORED CHAIN CLIENT IS A FORK FROM DAY ONE AND NOTHING FULLY GUARDS IT. core/lib/chain-read.js is adapted, not copied: different koilib minor, no signer parameter, half the methods removed. No checksum test can cover an adapted file. The runtime address-mismatch log catches the specific failure that shows a wrong number on screen; it does not catch a semantics change in how koilib serialises a call. Mitigation is the header comment naming the counterpart in both directions and the fact that this code changes roughly never — not a guarantee.

3. /core/* REMAINS UNAUTHENTICATED AFTER STAGE 1. Stage 1 adds no money route, so nothing new can be spent — but GET /core/koinos does expose the watch address and the node RPC URL to any local process and to a cross-origin POST from any page the user visits, exactly like every other /core route today. The Origin check is stage 2's entry price, which means it lands after this data is already reachable. Acceptable because the data is an address the user chose to type and a URL, but it is a real widening.

4. THE CAPABILITY THRESHOLDS ARE DUPLICATED FACTS THAT ROT SILENTLY. arch, RAM, disk, the install-path probe, the releases URL and the cost prose all mirror things that live in the other repo. The day arm64 Koinos images ship, Koinos AI tells Pi users a lie until someone edits one line. Making the requirements a data object with a "last verified" date rendered in the card reduces the blast radius; it does not remove it. Realistically this is the item most likely to be wrong in eighteen months.

5. THE HARBINGER CI TEST PROVES THE TRANSACTION PATH, NOT THE MAINNET ENVIRONMENT. It exercises signing, nonce and the burn/register call shapes with free tokens, which is the right gate. It will not reliably reproduce mainnet mana pressure or fee conditions, and a burn sized at the exact limit reverts opaquely on chain. The local mana cushion is the real defence; the test only confirms the cushion is applied.

6. THE FEATURE IS STILL VALUELESS TO A NON-CRYPTO USER, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. Flip the toggle with no node and no KOIN and you get a capability verdict, an empty watch field and a link to another app. Refusing the onramp is correct engineering — a partial one strands real ETH — but the honest consequence is that the switch does nothing for the majority of an AI app's audience. The mitigation is that it costs them nothing to find out: off is inert, on is free, and the toggle's own sub-line says what it does before they flip it.

7. THE STAGE ORDER BETS THAT BURN AND REGISTER MATTER MORE THAN SEND. If the owner's actual users mostly want to move KOIN between wallets rather than convert it to VHP, stage 3 should be stage 2. The safety prerequisites are identical either way, so this is a re-ordering, not a redesign — but I have put the node-operator operations first because that is what a feature called "node tools" is for.

## Grafted from the losing design

- FROM NODE CONSOLE — the whole structural spine: vendor only chain facts (a read-only chain client, network constants, two ABIs, ~25 KB) and refuse to share, package or re-implement anything else. Zero new npm dependencies; koilib 9.3.0 is already a top-level kaiapp dependency and I confirmed it exports utils, Contract, Signer, Provider, Transaction, Serializer, Multicall.
- FROM NODE CONSOLE — the capability gate runs BEFORE any install or start control is drawn, from core/lib/hardware.js (which already reports process.platform, process.arch, os.totalmem() and statfs freeBytes). Nobody downloads anything to discover their chip is unsupported.
- FROM NODE CONSOLE — the watch-address field. It decouples 'can this computer run a node' from 'can this computer show me my node', which turns the Pi and every low-RAM laptop from a refusal card into a working dashboard for the $6-15/mo x86_64 VM the owner already ships provisioning scripts for.
- FROM NODE CONSOLE — the onramp verdict: no onramp code, no sponsor key, no ethers in Koinos AI. This is simultaneously the largest security win and the reason the @noble/hashes 2.3.0 subpath break (which kills eth.js at require time, not call time) can never fire.
- FROM KOINOS MODE — the node connection card. One field, default http://127.0.0.1:8080, probed with chain_get_head_info: connected / head / blocks behind. Koinos Node Desktop already serves exactly that via its always-on jsonrpc profile. ~60 lines, and it is the only part of stage 1 that serves the person who already runs a node.
- FROM KOINOS MODE — the memory-saver footnote in that card: a node in saver mode drops the jsonrpc profile, which is the same service serving localRpcUrl. The card must say 'your node is running but its status port is off in memory-saver mode', not 'not connected', or it reads as a Koinos AI bug.
- FROM KOINOS MODE — one real privacy chokepoint. Gateway.prototype._requireEgress(res, feature), used by the new routes AND retrofitted onto the six retyped copies I confirmed at gateway.js:278, 287, 395, 655, 720, 770, plus a test that enumerates the new routes and asserts each is inert in Local-Only.
- FROM KOINOS MODE — rewrite the UI, never paste renderer.js, for four independently verified reasons (the const $ collision throws a SyntaxError that kills the entire file; 138 inline style attributes are dropped by kaiapp's style-src 'self'; .view/.active is the inverse of kaiapp's hidden attribute; 12 class tokens, two pulse keyframes and the --accent tokens collide destructively). Treat renderer.js as the spec for what the screens SAY.
- FROM KOINOS MODE — the pre-flip hardware honesty line, computed and shown under the switch before anyone commits to anything.
- FROM BOTH JUDGES — kill the ABI-hash drift test in favour of a runtime log-once when resolveContracts() returns an address different from the vendored fallback. Three lines, no cross-repo coupling, and it guards the thing that can actually be wrong on screen.
- FROM BOTH JUDGES — replace precise cost numbers that rot (200 MB / 60 GB / 160 GB / 2-4 GB) with ranges plus a link to koinos-node's README as the single source of truth.

## Core routes

- GET /core/koinos — the single poll route, mirroring GET /core/earn. Returns flat-spread {ok:true, enabled, capable:{ok,reason,detail,requirementsVerified}, companion:{installed,version}, node:{configured,rpcUrl,connected,head,behind,note}, chain:{watchAddress,koin,vhp,mana,manaMax,rechargeEta}|null, privacy:{mode,chainReadsAllowed}}. NOT koinos-node's nested {ok,data}. When koinos.enabled is false it returns {ok:true, enabled:false} and nothing else — no probe, no Provider, no fetch.
- POST /core/koinos/config — accepts ONLY {enabled?, watchAddress?, nodeRpcUrl?}. No companionPath, no filesystem path of any kind, no password. Mirrors /core/earn/config and /core/network/config.
- INTERNAL — Gateway.prototype._requireEgress(res, feature): the one chokepoint replacing the six retyped copies of `const mode = this.network ? this.network.status().privacyMode : "local-only"` I confirmed at gateway.js:278, 287, 395, 655, 720, 770. Land the helper and the retrofit with this feature.
- INTERNAL — one prefix guard `if (this.koinos && path.startsWith("/core/koinos"))` placed alongside the earn block at gateway.js:592. Put the handlers in their own core/lib/koinos-routes.js module rather than inline: _route is already a ~530-line linear if-ladder and is the app's worst maintenance hotspot.
- ELECTRON — ipcMain 'companion:open' (resolves the path from Core itself, spawns detached) and 'companion:pick' (a file picker for the Linux AppImage case). Exposed as window.koinosShell.openCompanion / pickCompanion. Deliberately NOT Core routes: a process-spawn route on an unauthenticated, CORS-simple-reachable control plane is reachable from any web page the user visits.
- STAGE 2 ADDS (each behind the Origin check, each requiring the wallet password in the body every call even while unlocked — the revealWif precedent at core/lib/wallet.js:248): GET /core/koinos/max-burn · POST /core/koinos/burn {amount,password} · GET /core/koinos/producer · POST /core/koinos/producer/register {publicKey,password} · GET /core/koinos/tx/:id
- STAGE 3 ADDS: POST /core/koinos/send {to,amount,password} — shipped on its own, after stage 2 has been live for a release.
- SEPARATE PR, BEFORE ANY OF THE ABOVE: an Origin / Sec-Fetch-Site check on the whole /core control plane. It touches every /core consumer in the app, so shipping it inside the wallet PR makes an email or MCP regression indistinguishable from a wallet bug at bisect time.

## Later stages

- STAGE 2 — WRITES THAT DON'T MOVE MONEY TO ANYONE ELSE: burn KOIN→VHP, and producer key registration. Blast radius is your own VHP, not a third party's wallet, which is why these come before send. ## Test plan

- STAGE 1 SPENDS NOTHING, BY CONSTRUCTION. There are no write routes, no password prompt and no signer in stage 1, so "test without spending real KOIN" is not a testing trick — it is a property of the design. This is the main reason to ship read-only first.
- INJECT THE PROVIDER, NEVER CONSTRUCT IT INSIDE THE SERVICE. core/lib/chain-read.js takes {provider} (or a factory) so every unit test passes a stub returning canned chain_get_account_rc / balance_of / chain_get_head_info responses. Zero network in `npm test`, no Docker, no daemon, and it runs on the CI box today.
- NO-EGRESS TEST, the one that must never be skipped: monkeypatch globalThis.fetch to throw on any call, set privacyMode local-only, poll GET /core/koinos, assert chainReadsAllowed:false, chain:null, and that fetch was never invoked. Repeat with koinos.enabled false in every privacy mode and assert {ok:true, enabled:false} with no other keys and again zero fetch calls. This is also the test that catches a forgotten _requireEgress on any future /core/koinos route.
- ROUTE ENUMERATION TEST: walk the /core/koinos handlers and assert every one is inert in local-only. gateway.js has six independently retyped privacy checks (lines 278, 287, 395, 655, 720, 770) and no test asserting routes are gated — this is the test that stops a seventh copy from being forgotten.
- CAPABILITY GATE, pure function: feed it fabricated {platform, arch, ramBytes, freeBytes} objects. arm64 → capable:false with the exact Pi reason string. x64 / 4 GB → capable:false, RAM reason. x64 / 16 GB / 60 GB free → capable:true, quickSync:false. x64 / 16 GB / 200 GB free → capable:true, quickSync:true. No hardware needed and it locks the copy in place.
- COMPANION DETECTION: point the probe at a temp directory containing a fake app bundle plus a package.json, assert installed:true and the parsed version; assert an unknown path yields installed:false and that nothing is ever spawned. Assert POST /core/koinos/config REJECTS a companionPath key outright — a regression test against re-introducing the spawn gadget.
- NODE RPC PROBE: stand up a plain node:http server in the test that answers chain_get_head_info with a canned height, point nodeRpcUrl at it, assert connected/head/behind arithmetic; then close it and assert the "nothing is answering" state including the memory-saver note. No Docker, no koinos images, ~30 lines.
- BROWSER TESTS in the shape of the existing core/test/earn-ui.browser.test.js: toggle reveals the Koinos nav item and #view-koinos; toggle off hides both; assert #view-koinos has a computed overflow-y of auto (the bug both candidate designs walked into); assert no element in the new markup carries a style attribute (the CSP is live — index.html:357 already ships one dead inline style). And re-run earn-ui.browser.test.js unchanged: it waits on #earn-setup:not([hidden]) and #earn-ready:not([hidden]) transitions, and inserting at line 376 is outside #earn-ready, so it must still pass untouched.
- CHAIN-FACT DRIFT: not an ABI hash test. Log once at runtime whenever resolveContracts() returns a contract address different from the vendored fallback in chain-constants.js, and assert in a unit test that the mismatch path fires. Three lines, no cross-repo coupling, and it guards the thing that can actually show a wrong number. The ABIs are the flattest-history artifacts here; the file that will genuinely diverge is the adapted chain-read.js, which no hash can cover — say so in its header comment instead.
- PACKAGING SMOKE TEST, cheap and currently missing: build.files is ["electron/**","core/**","ui/**","!core/test/**","package.json"] and there is no packaged-app check, so anything new must live under those globs. Add a test asserting core/lib/abi/*.json is present relative to __dirname at runtime — a vendored JSON silently absent from the installer is the classic bad release for a solo maintainer.
- KOILIB SINGLETON ASSERTION: assert require.resolve("koilib") from core/lib/chain-read.js equals the one core/lib/wallet.js resolves. Nothing shares a Signer today, but kaiapp already carries "overrides": {"protobufjs": "7.4.0"} — direct evidence this stack has been bitten by duplicate-dependency breakage once, and a second koilib instance in a money path is the kind of bug that costs a day.
- STAGE 2 ENTRY GATE — HARBINGER, NOT MAINNET, NOT MOCKS. Before any write route ships: a CI job that, on every push, runs a real burn and a real producer registration against Koinos harbinger using a throwaway key held as a repository secret. Harbinger tokens are free, so no real KOIN is ever spent, and it exercises the true signing, nonce and mana path that a stub cannot. Assert the mana cushion by attempting a burn sized at the exact limit and asserting the app refuses it locally rather than letting the chain revert opaquely. Writes do not ship without this job green — at v0.3.7→v0.27.5 in four days, a per-release manual money pass will not survive week one.
- STAGE 2/3 ALSO: an Origin-check test (a POST with a foreign Origin header, and one with no Origin, must both be refused on money routes) and a password-required test (every write route rejects a body without the correct password even while wallet.status().unlocked is true).

- DON'T tell users to send KOIN to the Koinos AI address. Verified: core/lib/wallet.js exposes no transfer and gateway.js has no transfer route, and core/server.js:222 confirms the scheduler operator pays mana, so the earn wallet needs no KOIN to work. That advice is an irreversible one-way deposit for zero benefit, with reveal-WIF-and-import-elsewhere as the only exit. Show the address as an identity with "Koinos AI can't send from this address."
- DON'T put mainnet balance tiles inside the same .api-card as the sentence "Alpha runs on the Koinos testnet — rewards are test tokens" (ui/index.html:307). Separate view, separate heading, and a standing "real KOIN" line that never disappears.
- DON'T accept companionPath — or any filesystem path — in a /core POST body. _authed() is never called under /core/ (only gateway.js:652/664/672/690), grep for origin/cors/referer returns zero matches, and bodies are JSON-parsed regardless of content-type, so a CORS-simple cross-origin POST from any visited page reaches the handler. A stored path that Electron later spawns is a remote-code-execution gadget with a delayed fuse.
- DON'T ship send, burn or producer-registration before all three of: the Origin/Sec-Fetch-Site check on the control plane, the wallet password in the body of every money route on every call, and a harbinger transaction test running in CI. At v0.3.7→v0.27.5 in four days, an untested mainnet write path ships broken on some release — the question is only which one.
- DON'T ship the catalog-managed install of Koinos Node Desktop. catalog.json sha256-pins every entry and fails closed on PENDING; koinos-node's release workflow publishes no checksums. That is a permanent two-repo release train for a download the user clicks through an unsigned-binary warning on anyway.
- DON'T make packages/ a workspace and require it by name. build.files is ["electron/**","core/**","ui/**","!core/test/**","package.json"] — it would resolve in development and be absent from the installer, and there is no packaged-app smoke test to catch it. If any sub-package ever exists, declare koilib as a peerDependency and assert a single resolved instance; the existing "overrides": {"protobufjs": "7.4.0"} is evidence this stack has already been bitten by a duplicated dependency.

Its top-level `const $` collides with app.js:10 and throws a SyntaxError that stops the whole file executing; the two helpers take different arguments (selector vs bare id), so a mechanical rewrite returns null at all 180 sites; its 138 inline style attributes are silently dropped by style-src 'self'; its .view/.active visibility is the inverse of kaiapp's hidden attribute; and 12 class tokens, two conflicting @keyframes pulse and the --accent/--accent-2 tokens collide destructively. Use kaiapp's self-rescheduling setTimeout gated on the view being visible (the app.js:1015 pattern), plus the userIsBusy() guard from tools-view.js:32-36 — the stronger one, written after a field report of a <select> resetting mid-choice, not the weaker overwrite-only-when-empty check in renderEarn.
